Corequisite Foundations Reference Manual

Key definitions, algebraic procedures, and common misconception traps aligned to MATH 017 Modules 1 & 3.
Module 1 · Topic A

Percentages Greater Than 100%

In statistics, rates exceeding baseline (such as relative risk, price indices, or percent growth) often exceed 100%.

Core Rule: Percent means per hundred. Divide by 100:
P% = P / 100
Because P ≥ 100, the resulting decimal is always ≥ 1.0 and the fraction is an improper fraction (numerator > denominator).
Common Trap: Writing 107% as 0.107 (moving 3 decimal places / dividing by 1000) or thinking "a percent cannot exceed 100". Remember: 100% is 1.0, so 107% is 1.07.
Module 3 · Topic B

Place Value Discrimination & Ordering

When comparing p-values or standard errors (e.g. 0.0408 vs 0.048 vs 0.0048), length of decimal does not determine size.

Systematic Alignment Method:
  1. Line up decimal points vertically.
  2. Pad trailing zeros to equalize lengths:
    0.0048
    0.0408
    0.0480
  3. Compare digits from left to right at the first place they differ.
Trap: Thinking 0.0048 is largest because 48 has two digits. In reality, the tenths and hundredths places both hold zero, making 0.0048 the smallest of the three!
Module 3 · Topic C

Rounding Small Proportions with Leading Zeros

In hypothesis testing and confidence intervals, small proportions like 0.003482 require exact place-value precision.

Rounding Checklist:
  • Tenths (1st place): Look at hundredths. For 0.003482 → 0.0
  • Hundredths (2nd place): Look at thousandths (3 < 5) → 0.00
  • Thousandths (3rd place): Look at ten-thousandths (4 < 5) → 0.003
  • Ten-thousandths (4th place): Look at 8 (≥ 5) → 0.0035
Crucial Statistical Fact: Writing 0.00 is NOT "just zero." The trailing zeros explicitly declare that the measurement was recorded with two decimal places of precision!
Module 1 · Topic D

Survey Tables & Relative Frequencies

Relative frequency expresses the proportion of occurrences in each category relative to the sample total.

Formulas:
Relative Frequency (Decimal) = Category Count / Total Sample (N)
Percentage (%) = Relative Frequency × 100%
Sum Check: ∑ Relative Frequencies = 1.000 (100.0%)
Note on Rounding: Due to individual rounding of decimals, the final sum may occasionally equal 0.999 or 1.001. In statistical reports, note this as "differences due to rounding."
Module 1 · Topic E

Proportional Scaling to Larger Populations

Projecting sample findings to an entire incoming cohort or target population using mathematical proportionality.

Three Equivalent Solution Pathways:
1. Cross-Multiplication:
x / n = X / N_pop ⇒ n · X = x · N_pop ⇒ X = (x · N_pop) / n
2. Unit Rate (Sample Proportion):
î = x / n ⇒ Projected = î × N_pop
3. Cohort Multiplier:
k = N_pop / n ⇒ Projected = x × k
Sanity Check: Always check if the projected count is reasonable relative to the sample proportion (e.g., if 40% of the sample did something, the projection must be slightly less than half of the cohort).
